openMemristorPDK · beginner's guide

Design with memristors, step by step

A hands-on guide to openMemristorPDK, a small chip-design kit for memristors built with openPDKcreator. Every step shows a real screenshot of what to click, from installing the tools to simulating a memristor and looking at its layout. No chip-design experience needed.

Before you start

What this guide is about

What is a memristor?

A resistor with a memory. Its resistance depends on how much current has flowed through it before, so it "remembers". Its fingerprint is the pinched hysteresis loop you will simulate in section 2.

What is a PDK?

A Process Design Kit: the instruction manual a chip factory gives designers. It says which materials and layers exist, how small things can be, and how each device behaves. openMemristorPDK describes a made-up factory that builds memristors.

How to read the steps

  • Click what is inside the red box.
  • Type exactly what is in a black box (or use its Copy button).
  • Play as video walks through a section on its own.
  • Click a screenshot to see it full size.

What you need

  • A Linux computer (Ubuntu or similar).
  • About 35 GB of free disk space and an internet connection, the first time.
  • No programming: every command is given.
Sections

Pick where to start

Do the sections in order the first time. Later ones build on the earlier ones.

SECTION 1

Install from scratch

Get a new Linux computer ready: two programs, openPDKcreator, the memristor PDK and the container with all the chip-design tools.

Ready30–60 min, mostly waiting · once per computer SECTION 2

Your first simulation

Start the tools, read the memristor's model, simulate its pinched hysteresis loop in xschem and ngspice, then look at its chip layout in KLayout.

Readyabout 10 min · 24 steps SECTION 3

Add a model

Write a new Verilog-A memristor model, let openPDKcreator compile it, and try it in the testbench in place of the old one.

Readyabout 15 min · 24 steps SECTION 4

Add a new device

Delete the Au/HfO₂/Al memristor and rebuild it from nothing: layers, layout, symbol, model and testbench.

Readyabout 15 min · 33 steps SECTION 5

Corners and Monte Carlo

Simulate the TT/FF/SS process corners, then a Monte Carlo run where Ron, Roff and the oxide thickness vary from device to device.

Readyabout 15 min · 41 steps SECTION 6

The digital side

Store a bit in a memristor and read it back, see what happens when a write is too weak, then a draft plan for memories built from memristors.
